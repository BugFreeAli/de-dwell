### A15 (2026-11-22): learned scorers against objective-aware planners at scale (plan written before any A15 code is committed)
Status: plan only. The freeze point is the commit that adds this text (workflow of 2026-11-17: no tags). No A15 run has been made on the author's machine when this text is committed.

**A15.1 Why, and what is already known (disclosure).** A14 gave the outcome MISMATCH: for the survival objective the exact expected-value optimum loses up to 32% to 38% of the optimal survival probability and objective-aware planning (RU_DPEV, one exact improvement step of DPEV) recovers most of it at n = 4 and 5, but RU_DPEV needs the exact expected-value optimum, which exists only for small n. The scalable objective-aware planners are CEPS and RU_CEP (A14.4). The title question of the project, whether a learned scheduler beats strong planners, has not been tested with a learner trained for this objective. The only earlier learner is the B0 imitation scorer (expected-value objective), which lost to exact rollouts. Xu and Mannor (2011) show that a goal-probability policy needs the accumulated value; this gives the ablation IL_G_noacc.
Exploratory design iterations, by me in my sandbox only (throwaway seeds 11 and 12, pool of 150 instances, 3 seeds per variant, 40 epochs, 3 informative cells at n = 5 with 72 instances and 2 at n = 7 and 9 with 32 and 20 instances; not results, not repeated on the author's machine): (1) a plain per-action scorer trained at n <= 5 with the exact goal teacher (IL_G) was 2.2 points behind RU_CEP at n = 5 (mean survival probability 0.529 against 0.551) and 12 to 21 points behind at n = 7 and 9; a scorer trained on the expected-value teacher (IL_EV) reached 0.512 at n = 5 and was above IL_G at n = 7 and 9; (2) clipping the base features to the range seen in training did not change this much; (3) giving the scorer the action recommended by CEPS as one input column (IL_A, trained on the exact goal teacher at n <= 5) reached 0.555 at n = 5 (regret against the exact optimum 3.7 points; CEPS 7.4, RU_CEP 4.4, DPEV 5.4, RU_DPEV 0.75), 0.762 at n = 7 (CEPS 0.798, RU_CEP 0.817) and 0.752 at n = 9 (CEPS 0.859, RU_CEP 0.873) with a large spread over seeds at n = 9 (SD 0.11); (4) a plain scorer distilled from RU_CEP at n = 6 and 7 (without the CEPS column) reached only 0.695 at n = 7 and 0.712 at n = 9. The design below was chosen after these observations (a forking path that is disclosed); the seeds 11 and 12 are not used again. Decision time in the sandbox (cold decision, mean): at n = 9 RU_CEP 320 ms, CEPS 8.1 ms, a scorer 0.2 ms.
Expectation stated before the run [OPEN]: LEARN-BEATS is unlikely; IL_A may improve on CEPS in distribution; LEARN-BEHIND is plausible at n = 9 because the training sizes are small.

**A15.2 Questions and hypotheses.** H15.1: a learned scorer that gets the CEPS recommendation and is trained on RU_CEP at larger sizes (IL_AD) matches RU_CEP within one point of survival probability at n = 7 and 9 at least 10 times faster (LEARN-MATCHES), or beats it (LEARN-BEATS). H15.2: the same scorer improves on the cheap planner CEPS. H15.3: the teacher objective matters for the learner (IL_G above IL_EV at n = 5). H15.4: the accumulated value is needed (IL_G above IL_G_noacc at n = 5). H15.5: the planner recommendation as an input helps (IL_A above IL_G at n = 5).

**A15.3 Instances and objective.** The survival objective, instances, hidden classes and exact evaluation of A14 (src/dwell/m0u.py); every method is evaluated exactly by enumerating all 2^n hidden-class vectors, no sampling. Reference U_opt (exact goal DP) only at n = 5. No exact reference exists at n = 7 and 9; no claim of optimality is made there.

**A15.4 Methods.** Planners (all sizes): CEP (expected-value rule), CEPS, RU_CEP (A14.4); at n = 5 also DPEV and RU_DPEV. Learned scorers, one hidden layer of 32 tanh units shared across candidate actions, softmax over the feasible actions, cross-entropy, Adam (learning rate 0.01, batch 512, 60 epochs), numpy only (src/dwell/learned_g.py). Features per action: the 12 base features of B0 clipped to the range met at the training sizes, 2 static goal features (need / W, remaining killable value / W), 6 accumulated-value features (need left / W, value over need left, value >= need left, surplus, value times success probability over need left, need left over remaining value), and for IL_A and IL_AD the CEPS recommendation (1 for the recommended action). Variants (10 training seeds each, 50 models): IL_G (columns 0 to 19, teacher exact goal optimum), IL_EV (same inputs, teacher exact expected-value optimum), IL_G_noacc (columns 0 to 13, no accumulated value, teacher exact goal optimum), IL_A (columns 0 to 20, teacher exact goal optimum), IL_AD (columns 0 to 20, teacher RU_CEP).
Training data: pool G = 500 instances (n in {4, 5}, rho in {1.1, 1.5}, s in {2, 4}, label accuracy in {0.5, 0.9}, leakage tolerance lam in {0.15, 0.25, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6}), pool seed 20261262; per instance 24 sampled hidden-class worlds followed by an epsilon-greedy teacher (epsilon cycling 0, 0.1, 0.25; the goal teacher and the expected-value teacher alternate); unique (state, accumulated value) pairs; labels from the exact goal DP (none where no action can still help) and from the exact expected-value DP. Pool D = 150 instances (n in {6, 7}, same other grids, instance seed pool seed + 1), 30 worlds, epsilon-greedy RU_CEP rollouts, labels from RU_CEP. Each model uses a random 70% of the instances of its pool (the seed decides the subset and the initialisation); the pools are shared across seeds.

**A15.5 Evaluation and statistics.** Sizes n = 5, 7, 9; cells rho in {1.1, 1.5}, s in {2, 4}, a in {0.5, 0.9}, lam in {0.25, 0.5} (16 cells per size); instances per cell 80, 50, 30; eval seed 20261261. A cell is informative if the mean U of RU_CEP lies in [0.10, 0.99]. Unit of inference: the training seed. For a variant and a size the seed-level value is the mean U over all instances of the informative cells; a contrast is the seed-level value of A minus that of B, where B is a planner (one number) or another variant paired by seed. Exact one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test (H1: difference > 0); Holm correction over the 8 primary contrasts at 0.05; material means mean difference >= 0.01 (one point of survival probability); a win is Holm-significant and material. Primary contrasts: IL_A against CEPS (n = 5); IL_AD against CEPS (n = 7, 9); IL_AD against RU_CEP (n = 7, 9); IL_A against IL_G (n = 5); IL_G against IL_EV (n = 5); IL_G against IL_G_noacc (n = 5). Everything else (other variants, the exact regret at n = 5, per-cell tables, instance level, spread over seeds) is descriptive. The test conditions on the fixed evaluation instances and measures training variability only; instance variability is not part of the p-values (limit).
Decision time: cold per-decision wall time (mean, ms) of IL_AD, CEP, CEPS and RU_CEP at n = 7 and 9 on 10 evaluation instances with up to 12 decisions each (scripts/run_a15.py time); the time of IL_AD includes the CEPS call.

**A15.6 Verification before any main run.** (1) tests/test_a15.py and tests/test_a15_analysis.py (26 tests): numerical gradient of the scorer, save and load, feature layout and clipping, accumulated-value columns, CEPS flag, policy stops at the goal, labels of the pools are exactly optimal (goal labels equal the exact maximiser and exist only where some action can help; expected-value labels equal the exact policy), epsilon-greedy rollouts, label choice and instance subsampling per variant, reproducibility, row invariants and planner sets by size, exact Wilcoxon values, Holm, informative cells, the contrasts, the decision table and its secondary flags. (2) scripts/mutate_a15.py: 19 mutations, all caught. (3) audit of the whole pipeline (pool, train, eval, time) on exploratory seeds. (4) scripts/a15_gate.py writes results/a15_gate.json with the SHA-256 of nine code files (learned_g.py, analysis_a15.py, m0u.py, learned.py, m0.py, dp.py, rules.py, instances.py, run_a15.py); run_a15.py refuses the role main without a gate that carries the current hash.

**A15.7 Procedure.** Commit this text. Add the code, run tests, mutations, the audit pipeline and the gate, commit. Then run on the main config with 4 workers, stage by stage and resumable: pool, train, eval, time; analyse with scripts/analyze_a15.py --dir results/a15_main. All rows, violations included, are kept; a failed stage is repeated with the same config. Cost not measured at main size [OPEN]: from the sandbox previews I expect minutes for the pool G stage, a longer pool D stage (RU_CEP labels at n = 6 and 7), minutes for training, and one to several hours for the evaluation; hedged total 2 to 4 hours on 4 workers.

**A15.8 Decision table (applied by script, first match).**
- NO OUTCOME: a size has no informative cell, or the decision times are missing where they are needed.
- LEARN-BEATS: IL_AD beats RU_CEP at n = 7 and at n = 9 (both contrasts are wins).
- LEARN-MATCHES: not BEATS; the mean difference IL_AD minus RU_CEP is at least -0.01 at n = 7 and at n = 9; and RU_CEP needs at least 10 times the decision time of IL_AD at n = 9. Meaning: quality-time frontier, the learned scorer replaces the rollout planner at comparable quality and far lower cost.
- LEARN-BEHIND: otherwise. Meaning: the objective-aware planners remain the better methods at this training budget; the learned scorers are reported as a negative result with their controls.
Secondary flags (descriptive): objective_matters (IL_G over IL_EV is a win), accumulated_value_needed (IL_G over IL_G_noacc), planner_feature_helps (IL_A over IL_G), improves_cheap_planner (IL_A over CEPS at n = 5 and IL_AD over CEPS at n = 7 and 9 are all wins).

**A15.9 Limits stated now.** n <= 9 and exact evaluation; teachers at n <= 5 (exact) and n = 6, 7 (planner); one architecture (imitation of one-step labels, not reinforcement learning, no search); IL_A and IL_AD contain the CEPS call, so their decision cost is that of CEPS plus a small network; the claim "learning" there is learning a correction of a planner; the training pools are shared across seeds; the design was fixed after sandbox previews (A15.1); the thresholds (one point, ten times, 0.10 to 0.99) are my choices; the RU_CEP comparison is against one planner family (A14); the p-values do not include instance variability; the time measurement is for Python code on the author's laptop and is cold-decision time; the amendments A1 to A15 are post-hoc relative to each other and no multiplicity correction is applied across amendments.
