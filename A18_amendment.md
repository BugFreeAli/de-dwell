### A18 (2026-11-26): the survival objective under a wrong dwell scale (plan written before any A18 code is committed)
Status: plan only. The freeze point is the commit that adds this text (workflow of 2026-11-17: no tags). No A18 main run has been made when this text is committed.

**A18.1 Why, and what is already known (disclosure).** A14 to A17 assume the planner knows the dwell of every hardness class exactly. The only model-error evidence in the record (A6 to A10) is for the expected-value objective. The question here is whether the confirmed planner claim of A16 and A17 (the survival-aware certainty-equivalent planner CEPS beats its expected-value counterpart CEP) survives when the planner's dwell model is wrong, and what the wrong model costs. The planners are the practical ones: they are built on the nominal model and do not know that it is wrong. Robust or Bayes-adaptive planners (the A6/A7 line) are not tested here.
Error size, from the record: Burley (AFIT 2012) dwell prediction error at 1.045 um has median 13%, quartiles 10 to 19%, maximum 46% (transcribed by eye, partly OCR-confirmed). With the nominal dwell D = (5, 10) an integer true dwell needs a scale in {0.6, 0.8, 1.2, 1.4}, so 0.8 and 1.2 (20%, about the upper quartile) and 1.4 (40%, near the maximum) are used; a 10% error is not representable.
Exploratory looks before this plan (not used as results): timing runs (seed 3, 4 to 5 instances per cell) and the audit run (seed 3, 56 rows, 12 cells, rho 1.5, s 2, a 0.5, lam 0.25), whose printed output I saw. In those 56 rows the informed planners were above the nominal ones at scale 0.8 and the intervals were far too wide to say more. Nothing else was looked at.
Expectation stated before the run [OPEN]: the cost of the wrong scale (informed minus nominal CEPS) is likely LARGE at scale 0.8 and plausibly at 1.2 and 1.4; P1 (the gain of CEPS over CEP) is uncertain and may fail at one or more scales, so MOSTLY or NOT ROBUST is plausible; for scale 0.8 an informed expected-value planner may beat an uninformed survival-aware one (KNOWLEDGE-WINS).

**A18.2 Model and planners.** The true dwell of class k is scale x D0[k]; deadlines, values, slew, labels and priors are those of the nominal instance (common random numbers across scales: the same instance index gives the same instance at every scale). The planner is built on the nominal model and sees only what is observable: time, current target, dwell given to each threat, which threats were killed. A killed threat is DEAD; an alive threat has its dwell as nominal progress; the nominal canonical form marks a threat DEAD when the nominal model says it cannot be killed by its deadline, which includes progress beyond the largest nominal dwell (a threat that survives more dwell than any nominal class needs is abandoned). Evaluation is exact: every hidden class vector is enumerated and a kill happens when the dwell given reaches the true dwell of its class (src/dwell/a18.py, prob_goal_true). Informed references CEP_T and CEPS_T are the same planners built on the true model and evaluated in it; at n = 5 also the exact goal optimum of the true model (U_opt_T), the exact expected-value optimum of the nominal model (DPEV) and its goal rollout (RU_DPEV), both evaluated in the true world.

**A18.3 Design.** Eval seed 20261291 (not used before), bootstrap seed 20261292. Base instance family of A14 to A16 (D0 = (5, 10), values in {1, 2, 3}). True scales: 1.0 (same-seed control), 0.8, 1.2, 1.4. Cells: rho in {1.1, 1.5}, s in {2, 4}, label accuracy in {0.5, 0.9}, lam in {0.25, 0.5} (16 cells per scale and size). Sizes and instances per cell: n = 5: 80 (exact level), n = 7: 80 and n = 9: 40 (level ru: CEP, CEPS, RU_CEP). 192 cells and 12800 rows in all. Informative cell: the mean survival probability of the informed planner CEPS_T lies in [0.10, 0.99] (a reference that does not depend on the planners under test; it is chosen per scale, so the informative cells differ between scales).

**A18.4 Claims and labels (fixed now).** Statements per true scale in {0.8, 1.2, 1.4} and size n in {5, 7, 9} (nine statements):
- P1(scale, n): the one-sided 95% lower bound of the mean of U_CEPS minus U_CEP (both nominal, evaluated with the true dwell) is at least 0.01.
- Label (P1 only), first match: NO OUTCOME (a statement has no informative cell); ROBUST (P1 holds in all 9); MOSTLY (in at least 7); NOT ROBUST. The control (scale 1.0) is reported and is not part of the label.
Secondary, reported with intervals and not part of the label: (a) cost of the wrong scale, mean of U_CEPS_T minus U_CEPS: SMALL if the upper bound is at most 0.03, LARGE if the lower bound is at least 0.03, else INCONCLUSIVE; (b) awareness against knowledge, mean of U_CEPS minus U_CEP_T: AWARENESS-WINS if the lower bound is at least 0.01, KNOWLEDGE-WINS if the upper bound is at most -0.01, else MIXED; (c) cheap planner, mean of U_RU_CEP minus U_CEPS: ADEQUATE if the upper bound is at most 0.03, ROLLOUT-WORTH if the lower bound is at least 0.03, else INCONCLUSIVE; (d) at n = 5 the mean regret of each method against U_opt_T, in points. Everything else is descriptive.

**A18.5 Statistics.** As in A16 and A17: instance-level bootstrap stratified by cell, 2000 resamples, one-sided 95% bounds, pooled mean over the informative cells of a scale and size. The label is a conjunction of nine bounds, each at one-sided 95%; no multiplicity correction is applied across statements or amendments (the conjunction is conservative for ROBUST, not for MOSTLY).

**A18.6 Verification before any main run.** tests/test_a18.py (20 tests): hand cases of the true-dwell evaluation (dwell twice and half the believed one, abandonment when the deadline cannot be met), scale 1 reproduces the exact evaluation for CEP, CEPS, RU_CEP and DPEV, the decision cache does not change the probability (independent uncached implementation), integer scale check, rows without violation (never above the true optimum, no kill completes after its deadline), the scale is really used, informed references recomputed independently on the true model, common random numbers across scales, analysis (bounds instead of point estimates, informed reference selects the cells, labels, control outside the label, secondary classes, regret table), job layout (192 cells, 12800 rows), gate and seed refusal. scripts/mutate_a18.py: 27 mutations, all caught. Audit on an exploratory seed (configs/a18_audit.json, seed 3). scripts/a18_gate.py writes results/a18_gate.json with the SHA-256 of nine code files (a18.py, analysis_a16.py, analysis_a18.py, m0u.py, m0.py, dp.py, rules.py, instances.py, run_a18.py); run_a18.py refuses the role main without a gate that carries the current hash.

**A18.8 What each label means (not commitments).** ROBUST: the planner claim of A16 and A17 holds for the practical nominal planner at every tested scale error. MOSTLY: the scales or sizes where P1 fails are reported as limits. NOT ROBUST: the claim holds for a correct dwell model only and the paper says so; the secondary classes then show whether knowing the dwell or knowing the objective matters more.
**A18.9 Limits stated now.** One common multiplicative error for all classes (no class-specific or random error); the error is fixed per instance and unknown to the planner (no learning of the scale during the episode); planners are nominal, with no robust or Bayes-adaptive planner; three scales and the base family only; planners only (no learned scorers); no exact reference at n = 7 and 9; informative cells are selected with the informed planner; the thresholds are those of A16 and A17; the amendments A1 to A18 are post-hoc relative to each other.
