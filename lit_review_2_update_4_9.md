## 4.9 Literature read for amendment A14 (2026-11-21)
Reading depth is stated per item. Search was not systematic [OPEN].

**Read in full [DOC]**
- Xu and Mannor, Probabilistic Goal Markov Decision Processes, Proc. IJCAI 2011, pp. 2046-2051 (6 pages, text and references). The criterion is the probability that the accumulated reward of a policy reaches a target V. Stated results: an optimal policy may depend on the accumulated reward (their Example 1: policies that ignore it are inferior), and randomisation does not improve the probability (Theorem 1); the decision problem is NP-hard by reduction from knapsack (Theorem 2); with integer rewards it is solved in time polynomial in horizon, states, actions and the reward bound by augmenting the state with the accumulated reward (Theorem 3); real rewards are handled by discretisation with a stated error (Theorem 4); the chance-constrained variant (maximise expected reward subject to a probability constraint) is also pseudo-polynomial and may need randomisation (Theorem 5). The authors say that, to their knowledge, the criterion had not been studied for finite-horizon or discounted MDPs. Use in our work: the A14 goal DP is this state augmentation; the result that the accumulated value must be in the policy explains why the expected-value optimum (DPEV) is not optimal for survival. It is a general MDP paper with no scheduling, deadlines or dwell.
- Merlis, Richard, Sentenac, Odic, Molina, Perchet, On Preemption and Learning in Stochastic Scheduling, ICML 2023 (PMLR 202), arXiv:2205.15695v3. Main text and appendices read up to the start of the proof for UCB-RR (Proposition C.4); the remaining proof was not read because the fetched text ended there. Setting: one machine, jobs of K types with unknown exponential mean durations, objective total flow time, benchmark FTPP (schedule by increasing true mean). Stated results: non-preemptive explore-then-commit and UCB algorithms have sublinear excess cost over FTPP and any non-preemptive algorithm pays about n times the longest type mean when the means are far apart (Proposition B.5) and of order n^1.5 when they are close (Proposition 5.4); preemptive round-robin variants avoid the dependence on the longest type, and the authors state that the proofs for preemption rely on memoryless durations. They note that early exploration mistakes cost more than late ones. Use in our work: closest learning-the-type result in scheduling; the objective (flow time), exponential durations and no deadlines or slew differ from ours, so no result transfers. It supports a qualitative point only: preemption makes probing cheaper.

**Abstract or snippet only, not read [REF]**
- Dean, MIT thesis (DSpace 1721.1/30154): stochastic scheduling to maximise expected value of jobs completed by deadlines, with bounded gain from adaptivity (abstract level). Possible explanation for the near-optimality of re-planned certainty-equivalent plans; not read, do not cite details.
- Antoniadis et al., Stochastic scheduling with Bernoulli-type jobs through policy stratification, arXiv:2505.03349: index policies are not near-optimal in general for this completion-time problem; policy stratification gives approximation schemes (abstract and intro snippets). Possible source of adversarial instance families; not read.
- Tercan and Meisen, "When does learning stop and exact search have to take over" (university news item, 2026): title seen only; not used.
